# Procedural voxel materials

`src/engine/render/ProceduralVoxelTextures.ts` owns the block-face pixel field. It produces deterministic 16×16 RGBA tiles in a one-row 38-slot atlas consumed by `public/atlas.json` and the mesher worker, so startup no longer loads block texture PNGs. Each material family has four pattern realizations (the base tile plus three generated variants), and `PROCEDURAL_VOXEL_TEXTURE_SEED` is the stable seed for the material family.

The field stack uses a warped, multi-band value-noise signal for broad material grouping, meso breakup, and pixel variation. Each material then applies an authored palette and structural rule: dirt, grass, and sand use clustered particulate color; cobblestone uses jittered stone cells and mortar; wood side uses vertical grain while wood top uses square growth rings; grass side keeps its directional green cap.

The mesher chooses a material variant from stable world coordinates plus face direction. Sand, dirt, cobblestone, leaf faces, and growth-ring faces can use all quarter-turns; grass sides remain upright, and wood side grain is limited to 0°/180°. Grass caps and wood tops can rotate independently from their constrained side faces. The choices are deterministic, so chunk remeshing does not change the pattern.

The green and alternate leaf tiles share a sparse binary cutout field. The alternate block keeps legacy id/name `leaves_maple` for save and gameplay compatibility, but its palette and hotbar icon are light white-pink cherry blossom. `BlockMaterial` alpha-tests the atlas so the leaf gaps are visible without moving leaves into the water/transparent mesh path. The generator is a direct port of `ref/procedural_leaves.html`: six authored leaf silhouettes are transformed, placed on a coarse lattice, and area-reduced from 32×32 to the atlas tile without per-texel random alpha. Leaf tiles alone use derivative-aligned, tile-clamped footprint filtering under minification; opaque atlas materials retain nearest sampling, while oblique leaf faces no longer collapse into camera-dependent diagonal bands. The mesher deliberately emits leaf-adjacent boundaries, including leaf-to-leaf and leaf-to-trunk faces, so depth-tested cutouts can reveal successive canopy layers instead of exposing only the outer shell. The voxel sun-shadow volume keeps leaf cells in a separate occupancy field; mixed and sparse bricks sample the same generated atlas alpha with the mesher's world-space variant and face rotation when the receiver is terrain or another non-leaf surface. Dense leaf-only bricks preserve three exact silhouette layers before switching to a smoothly interpolated measured-density aggregate. Interior leaf receivers exclude cells sharing their reconstructed boundary and use smooth leaf-on-leaf transmission afterward, avoiding unstable projections between near-parallel binary cutout faces while retaining compounded canopy shade.

`createProceduralVoxelAtlas()` accepts a reproducible seed and `final`, `field`, or `mask` debug mode. The latter modes expose the controlling field and cutout mask for local material validation; the normal game uses `final`. `grass_leaves.png` and `seaweed.png` remain authored alpha-card assets by design.
