# Water

Water is intentionally split into gameplay data and visual presentation.

## Authoritative water blocks

The terrain generator and `World` own water blocks at `WATER_LEVEL`. These blocks participate in collision, swimming, ray selection, mining, placing, save files, and flooded-air tracking. `InteractionSystem` can fill a newly opened surface cell and propagate a thin water surface through connected cavities.

## Rendered water

`WaterSurfaceMaterial` shades water faces that come from voxel chunk meshes. Render-only optics use the visual center:

```text
VISUAL_WATER_LEVEL = WATER_LEVEL + OCEAN_WATER_CENTER_OFFSET
```

The half-block offset is a visual envelope for refraction, caustics, and screen-space masking; it does not change the gameplay interaction plane.

When `USE_OCEAN_HORIZON` is enabled, `WaterSystem` adds the far ocean surface and its visual seabed extension. These meshes never enter `World`, so they cannot affect collision or swimming, be selected, mined, placed on, or serialized into a save. The island generator is radial while gameplay bounds are rectangular, so render-only samples outside those bounds are clamped below the lowest wave trough; a generated land-height sample must never cross the optical interface. The one-block ring preserves the voxel boundary, while the far representation averages coarse samples into a continuous heightfield and closes both its inner seam and outer perimeter with skirts.

The gameplay/extension boundary follows a complementary-face contract. Authoritative edge chunks expose their outward faces when they are taller; when an outside extension column is taller, the extension samples the authoritative neighbour height and emits the missing inward wall one voxel at a time. The far closure skirts remain visible in direct rendering and in the water-free color/depth capture because they are real boundary faces. Their 16-block LOD edges are split into one-world-block wall segments with local U coordinates, preserving the same sand-tile density on outer sides as in the near voxel ring. Hiding any of these faces opens the seabed topology and makes its top surface appear as a hanging plane through the water.

The extension extracts the sand tile from the live voxel atlas and uses the same nearest, no-mipmap sampling response and base ambient visibility as authoritative sand. Its lighting, material response, shadows, and caustics remain synchronized with the shared block material. Real water-path extinction can still change sand appearance with depth, but crossing the invisible gameplay boundary alone must not change its base material.

## Optical inputs and frame behavior

`WaterSystem` receives the composer’s scene-color texture, shared depth texture, current voxel sun-visibility texture, camera matrices, sun direction/color, atmosphere colors, and caustic state. During that scene capture it hides both the render-only ocean and off-level voxel-water material. Sea-level water geometry is already omitted by the chunk mesher because the continuous ocean owns that interface.

The water-free capture intentionally omits voxel sun-shadow sampling to avoid a render-target feedback loop. Its alpha channel preserves each terrain receiver’s direct-light fraction. The final water shader therefore samples the current-frame visibility mask at the resolved refracted UV, reconstructs uncertain mask edges with depth-aware neighbours, and attenuates only that stored direct-light share. This makes character and terrain shadows remain visible on submerged sand through the interface without darkening ambient light or smearing shadows across shoreline and block-depth discontinuities.

The macro surface is a deterministic discrete spectral cascade: broad primary and crossing swell lobes, an oblique long-period secondary swell, and increasingly spread wind-sea bands. Energy is distributed across close-frequency pairs so their interference forms slow, irregular wave groups, while no single component can draw a dominant ruler-straight crest train. Every component uses finite-depth gravity/capillary dispersion, a unique phase, and an incommensurate wavelength. The CPU height query, vertex displacement, analytic fragment normal, and Jacobian foam signal consume the visible `OCEAN_WAVES` components. Their absolute amplitudes are normalized so even the theoretical all-in-phase sum stays within `-0.5 ... +0.5` blocks around `VISUAL_WATER_LEVEL`. Both vertex displacement and fragment normals now use a stable undeformed-base-plane pixel footprint with broad, overlapping band cutoffs. The inner grid and four horizon strips share exact boundary coordinates and matched first radial cells, while the outer-only lattice covers the widescreen far-plane corner envelope rather than stopping at axial camera-far distance; its coarser spacing remains below the resolved long-wave budget. The sun-lobe filter uses analytic spectral curvature instead of raster normal derivatives, so mesh-density changes cannot produce a bright horizontal LOD seam. Fragment optics also reconstruct the displaced carrier from the interpolated base plane with the same shared wave function, keeping view, depth, refraction, and BRDF inputs independent of triangle interpolation. A lower-energy seven-band detail spectrum and three advected gradient fields break up sub-grid facets without changing the bounded geometry envelope. The caustic projection uses the separate `OCEAN_CAUSTIC_WAVES` short-slope spectrum because those unresolved bands are optical transport input, not visible mesh displacement. Their wave vectors have integer X/Z cycle counts over the bounded caustic tile; the incommensurate macro waves are not baked into that repeating target because doing so would create a hard optical seam at every wrap.

Above water, the shader refracts the view ray with water’s index of refraction and projects that transmitted ray back through the active camera. Each projected receiver is checked against scene depth, the water interface, and its matching ray cone. When full refraction crosses a shoreline or foreground silhouette, a five-tap segment search walks back toward the incident pixel. Receiver validity remains derivative-smoothed at depth discontinuities and acts as soft distortion coverage: valid samples retain more projected displacement, while rejected samples blend toward the original incident screen pixel. A failed screen-space lookup therefore keeps the physical Fresnel split and never creates extra sky reflection. The resolved underwater distance drives Beer-Lambert extinction and in-scatter. Exact unpolarized dielectric Fresnel owns the energy split, with the geometric carrier surface providing a conservative filtered Fresnel floor when sub-pixel normals tilt toward a grazing camera. Because bounded screen-space refraction becomes ill-conditioned at the horizon, its transmission coverage fades out only over that reflection-dominated grazing interval. Debug mode 7 exposes projection coverage, soft receiver coverage, and grazing transmission coverage.

Direct sun glitter uses normalized GGX/Smith core and skirt slope lobes with analytic spectral curvature, finite solar-disc variance, and local roughness derived from the shared unresolved slope field. Quiet facets stay sharp while busy patches broaden and dim the lobe, creating the broken, uneven glint fragments seen on real water without adding an unrelated color mask or energy. The reflected sky carries only the atmospheric sun halo so a duplicate needle-like disc cannot cut through that path. Water debug modes 8 and 9 expose slope/roughness/glint response and footprint/band retention respectively.

Coastal foam is terrain-gated but never emitted from the shoreline mask alone. It requires both a sparse world-space patch and a wave-arrival signal derived from the shared displaced height, crest, and Jacobian fold. There is no unconditional residual swash term: that term read as a thin, continuous blue-white outline after final compositing instead of as physical foam.

The ocean surface uses one camera-medium authority for the entire interface draw. `WaterSystem` classifies the camera directly against the live displaced wave height at its XZ position, with no clearance band, pitch threshold, or face-orientation test. This is deliberately draw-wide: a displaced sheet can expose both raster face orientations around crests and during a crossing, but those fragments must not mix air-to-water and water-to-air optics in one frame. The actual surface geometry supplies the moving per-pixel waterline, while every fragment on the underwater side stays on the Snell-window path even when the window itself is outside the current view. That path has no boolean TIR material branch: it always evaluates a numerically safe transmitted direction, while exact Fresnel and derivative-filtered critical-angle coverage continuously reduce above-water transmission to zero outside the physical window. Total internal reflection remains an energy result around the window rather than a second material mode, and above-water sun radiance is evaluated only inside the Fresnel-weighted transmitted source.

Caustics are a bounded differential-area transport field, not a decorative line overlay. `WaterCaustics` projects a guarded regular surface patch through the live periodic optical wave normals and Snell refraction to a virtual receiver at `CAUSTIC_REFERENCE_DEPTH`; each texel stores `oldProjectedArea / newProjectedArea`, encoded by `CAUSTIC_FIELD_SCALE` so both float and byte render targets preserve a neutral mean of one. Its 53 m chunk-incommensurate domain uses twelve directional optical bands across a 1.5–17 m wavelength range, preventing the concentrated part of one short realization from repeating as square lit patches and quiet gaps. The guard band covers slope-displaced boundary rays, any untouched fallback texel is neutral rather than black, and a linear irradiance mip chain preserves the resolvable part of the field across grazing receiver footprints. Terrain and the visual seabed project their receiver position back to that same reference depth before sampling, so the pattern remains physically aligned at different seabed heights. The receiver replaces only direct sunlight with Fresnel transmission, Beer-Lambert light-path attenuation, receiver-angle response, and the sampled concentration; ambient, stars, albedo, and shadows remain owned by the normal block-lighting path. The underwater medium uses the same field only as a bounded modulation of direct single-scatter sunlight and filters it toward neutral irradiance when a ray segment or screen footprint cannot resolve the filaments.

The underwater medium pass stays active on both sides of the interface. It resolves the below-water segment per view ray and applies Beer-Lambert extinction plus eight world-space samples of particulate density, sky irradiance, sun irradiance, and a relative Henyey-Greenstein phase response. There is no camera-height fade: a ray receives the full optical effect of its actual water distance, which naturally tends to zero as an upward ray approaches the interface. This makes the transition continuous through ray geometry instead of cross-fading the dry render into a blue-tinted intermediate mode. Scattering remains non-zero at depth, so the view converges toward blue-green water airlight instead of a black vacuum; slow world-anchored noise supplies suspended sediment variation without screen-locked fog. Density frequencies are integrated against the ray-segment and pixel footprints, preventing the fixed samples from appearing as diagonal or camera-radiating slices. Aerial perspective is segment-aware and integrates only the air portion of a ray, leaving the water portion to this pass rather than applying atmospheric extinction twice. Its horizon airlight also requires a non-zero air segment. Both fullscreen medium passes consume the same displaced-surface camera-medium authority and camera-local live wave height as the interface shader. Their visible rays begin at the camera near plane: if an interface crossing lies in the clipped camera-to-near interval, that pixel's starting medium is toggled before either air or water is integrated. This lets dry and wet pixels coexist continuously while the near plane straddles a crest, without tinting the dry half from a hidden sub-near water segment. Opaque ocean pixels encode their visible normalized linear view depth in a reserved low-alpha range through the first post-process stages. Aerial perspective and the underwater medium therefore use the exact displaced interface that won each pixel instead of substituting the nominal plane or the water-free capture's seabed depth. The underwater pass skips these already-mixed pixels when the camera is above the surface. Above-water far-depth pixels without the interface marker are never treated as submerged receivers. Bloom preserves this internal alpha range until lens occlusion consumes it. Above water, the unrefracted geometric flare cannot treat a visible water pixel as open sky; underwater, the entire flare graph uses the analytically refracted source inside the Snell window and clears its prior-coordinate temporal history at a medium change. The Snell shader's Fresnel-weighted transmitted sun lobe remains the visible source at the interface. These rules prevent post-composite depth, medium extinction, or lens optics from bypassing the surface shader's `(1 - Fresnel)` transmission weight.

`SoundEffects` uses the active gameplay camera for underwater ambience and the authoritative player/world state for water-step audio. The camera rule is shared by first- and third-person views: a 50% submersion threshold across a small camera envelope starts the underwater loop and dims ocean ambience, while the ocean horizon remains render-only.
